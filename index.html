Bookkeeping for Garden Centres and Plant Nurseries: Seasonal Stock, Cafés and Grown-Your-Own

A garden centre sells living stock on a seasonal clock, with a café, a gift section and delivery vans attached. Bookkeeping that follows the seasons is what separates a good spring from a confusing one.

Living stock and seasonality

Plants bought in, grown on and sold — or lost to frost and disease — are stock with a short temper. Value unsold stock at year end at what it cost or what it can still fetch, whichever is lower, and write off dead stock when it dies, not at the next count. Seasonal buying (spring bedding, Christmas trees) should sit in the months it sells through.

Departments: plants, dry goods and the café

The café, the gift and homeware shelves and the plant area have different margins and different VAT stories. Till departments or separate cost codes per area keep each margin visible; one blended figure hides the department that is quietly losing.

Grown stock and propagation

Plants you propagate yourselves carry the cost of compost, pots, heat and time rather than a purchase invoice. A simple costing per batch keeps grown stock valued consistently with bought-in stock at year end.

Delivery and planting services

Delivery charges, planting and garden-tidy services are service income on top of the goods. Vehicle costs, seasonal staff wages through payroll, and polytunnel repairs belong in the cost lines they serve.
